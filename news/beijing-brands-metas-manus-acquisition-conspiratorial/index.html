Beijing brands Meta's Manus acquisition as 'conspiratorial' and bars founders from leaving China

The geopolitical dimension of AI development has escalated sharply.

The geopolitical dimension of AI development has escalated sharply. Chinese authorities have reportedly classified Meta's planned acquisition of Manus as conspiratorial and barred the company's founders from leaving China — a direct assertion of state control over AI talent and technology transfer.

Manus emerged in early 2026 as one of the most capable autonomous AI agent platforms globally, drawing attention from major US technology companies. Meta's reported acquisition interest triggered an immediate response from Beijing, which views frontier AI development as a national strategic asset rather than a commercially transferable technology.

The travel restriction on founders is an unusual and aggressive escalation. It signals that China's government is willing to use hard administrative tools — not just regulatory friction — to prevent AI talent and intellectual property from migrating to US firms. The message to other Chinese AI founders is equally clear.

For Western technology companies, the implications are significant. Acquisitions of frontier AI capabilities developed in China now carry a level of political risk that standard M&A frameworks do not account for. Due diligence must treat state intervention as a primary deal risk, not a tail scenario.

The broader dynamic underscores that the AI race is increasingly a geopolitical contest. Talent, models, and infrastructure are all being treated as strategic national resources — and both sides of the competition are willing to act on that framing.
