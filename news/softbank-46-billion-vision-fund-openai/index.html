SoftBank posts $46 billion gain at Vision Fund driven mainly by massive OpenAI bet

May 13, 2026 · Business

SoftBank's Vision Fund recorded a $46 billion gain in fiscal year 2025, primarily driven by its early investment in OpenAI — one of the most significant returns in venture capital history.

SoftBank's Vision Fund just recorded a $46 billion gain in fiscal year 2025, with the overwhelming majority driven by its early investment in OpenAI. This represents one of the most significant returns in venture capital history and signals a dramatic validation of the artificial intelligence opportunity that venture investors have been betting on for years.

The magnitude of this gain cannot be overstated. SoftBank's conviction in OpenAI during its early stages—when the company was far less proven than it is today—has generated returns that dwarf most traditional venture portfolios. This result underscores why investors continue to pour capital into AI companies at valuations that would have seemed unthinkable just five years ago. The Vision Fund's strategy of making large, concentrated bets on transformational technology is showing its merit.

What this means for investors and corporations is clear: timing and conviction matter enormously in venture investing. SoftBank's willingness to take a meaningful position in OpenAI while others were skeptical has created generational wealth. This success will likely inspire both copycat behavior and a fresh round of AI funding, as institutional investors reassess their exposure to artificial intelligence companies.

For the broader AI industry, this outcome is validating. OpenAI's trajectory from research organization to trillion-dollar-adjacent valuation demonstrates the real commercial value of advanced AI capabilities. Companies building on top of AI infrastructure, developing AI applications, and investing in AI talent will continue attracting capital at unprecedented levels.

Looking ahead, expect Vision Fund 2.0 and competitors to double down on AI investments. The question for most investors isn't whether to invest in AI anymore—it's which AI companies offer the best risk-adjusted returns going forward. SoftBank has shown the blueprint works.
